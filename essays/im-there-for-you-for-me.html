I'm there for you for me

Helping others becomes second nature once you understand the value attached to it. It is not merely philanthropy that draws me towards people; somewhere within it is also a desire to make a difference in my own life.

People often think that I am helping them solely for their sake. But at the back of my mind, I know that every time I help someone, I take something away from the experience. Nothing I do is truly free. The unspoken trade-off is always what I learn from the person I am supposedly helping.

Once, I was helping a friend prepare for NEET PG or rather, I was tasked with listening while she revised aloud. I knew almost nothing about medicine, yet, while listening to her, I began picking up fragments of human anatomy: how the heart regulates its rhythm, how nerves carry messages through the body, how organs quietly compensate for one another, and how a tiny malfunction can disturb an entire living system. I came to understand how splendid, and frighteningly fragile the human body is. She may have needed an attentive listener, but I was receiving an education in return.

And perhaps this is true of every person I have helped. From one, I learn patience; from another, resilience. Someone else teaches me how grief hides behind ordinary conversation, or how desperation can disguise itself as anger. Even a person’s mistakes become lessons that I can quietly carry away with me.

So, rather than thinking of myself as someone who gives without receiving, I have begun to see these encounters as exchanges. The other person may walk away with relief, advice, companionship, or simply the comfort of having been heard. I walk away with a better understanding of people and sometimes, with a better understanding of myself.

Yet this thought does not always make me feel good.

There is something grim about recognising profit within kindness. It makes me question whether I am actually compassionate or simply another kind of opportunist, someone who collects lessons from other people’s difficulties. When I sit beside someone in pain, am I truly there for them, or am I also studying their wounds, storing their experiences, and using them to make myself wiser?

Perhaps selfishness does not always arrive with cruelty. Sometimes, it wears a gentle face. Sometimes, it listens patiently, offers its shoulder, and says all the right things. That possibility unsettles me.

But maybe kindness and selfishness are not always opposites. Perhaps most human actions live somewhere in the uncomfortable space between them. I can genuinely want another person to heal while also knowing that their story will change me. My reasons may not be pure, but the help can still be real. A hand extended for complicated reasons may still pull someone out of the dark.

That is the dichotomy I live with: I am there for people because they need me, but also because I need what being there does to me. It gives me meaning. It teaches me. It makes my own life feel less hollow.

I am there for you but, if I am being completely honest, I am there for me too.

Written by Piyush Yadav
