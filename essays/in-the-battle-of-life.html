In the Battle of Life, Every Day Is a Fight

Life is what you make of it—or so we are told. There is some truth in that. But making something beautiful out of life is not easy. Beauty demands a price, and life rarely tells us how much it will take beforehand. It requires strength, courage, sacrifice and, above all, the will to continue when continuing begins to feel pointless.

For me, I have always been deeply influenced by hustle culture. Work until you secure survival, study until your eyes hurt, and continue until your body refuses to cooperate. Rest can wait. Happiness can wait. There is always another examination to prepare for, another opportunity to chase and another version of myself that I must become before I can feel worthy of anything.

I have lived, breathed and experienced this for years.

There is something dangerously comforting about struggle. It gives even an empty day a sense of purpose. Waking up tired feels meaningful when you believe you are suffering for a better future. Sleepless nights begin to resemble courage. Exhaustion starts looking like ambition. Slowly, you become proud of how badly you can treat yourself in the name of success.

You convince yourself that every sacrifice will eventually be repaid.

But life does not always honour such bargains.

Sometimes, months of preparation end with a result that does not carry your name. Sometimes, the application over which you spent several anxious nights receives nothing more than an automated rejection. Sometimes, you work harder than you have ever worked before and still remain exactly where you started.

The world does not pause to acknowledge how much the attempt cost you. It simply moves ahead.

That is perhaps the cruellest part of this battle: you can do everything right and still lose. There may be no lesson hidden in it, no immediate reward and no comforting explanation. Sometimes, failure is simply failure, and you are left alone to give meaning to the ruins.

And every attempt carries a cost.

While I remain busy preparing for the life I want, the life I already have continues to decay quietly around me. Messages remain unanswered until people stop sending them. Friends stop calling after hearing “I am busy” too many times. Family gatherings become interruptions rather than memories. Hobbies that once made me feel alive are buried because they are not productive enough.

There have been books I wanted to read but never opened because they were not part of any syllabus. There have been conversations I ended early because I had something “more important” to do. Meals have gone cold beside open books, and time with people has repeatedly been postponed with the same promise: once this examination is over, once this work is finished, once I finally get somewhere, I will have time.

But something else always begins.

Years pass like this. The examination changes, the deadline changes and sometimes even the dream changes, but the waiting remains. Life becomes a room outside which I continue to stand, telling myself that I will enter only after I have earned the right to live it.

There are days when I look back and struggle to remember what exactly I was running towards. I remember the deadlines, the failures and the constant fear of falling behind. But I do not always remember the person I was before all of them. In trying to build myself, I have sometimes reduced myself to a list of incomplete tasks.

My worth slowly became attached to how much I completed during the day. If I worked well, I felt useful. If I rested, I felt guilty. Even a peaceful evening began to feel like stolen time. Somewhere along the way, I stopped asking whether I was happy and began asking only whether I had been productive.

Perhaps struggle is necessary, but worshipping struggle is dangerous.

Not every wound is proof of progress. Not every sleepless night is heroic. Sometimes, exhaustion is simply exhaustion. Sometimes, pain does not make you stronger; it only makes you accustomed to being in pain. And sometimes, what looks like determination from the outside is merely fear—the fear that if you stop moving, you will finally notice how much of yourself you have already left behind.

Yet I cannot pretend that I have learned how to walk away from the fight.

Every disappointment takes something from me. Perhaps only a little, but enough to make the next attempt heavier. Still, somewhere at the back of my mind, a small and stubborn hope survives. It tells me that the next time may be different. So, I gather whatever confidence is still left and begin again.

I return to the same desk, the same books and the same uncertain future.

Not because I am fearless, but because surrender frightens me more.

Maybe courage is not an endless supply of motivation. Maybe it is simply waking up without it and continuing anyway. It is submitting another application after being rejected, opening the book after failing an examination and rebuilding a plan that life has already torn apart. It is carrying doubt every day without allowing it to completely take control.

In the battle of life, every day is a fight. But failure is not always the real enemy. Sometimes, the enemy is the voice that tells us that one failure has defined our entire existence. Sometimes, it is the hunger to succeed that slowly consumes everything our success was supposed to protect. And sometimes, it is the belief that life will only become meaningful once it becomes impressive.

I still want to achieve something beautiful. I still want my years of effort and sacrifice to mean something. But I am beginning to understand that life cannot remain postponed until the day I finally succeed.

The frightening possibility is that the promised day may never arrive.

And if it does arrive, I may discover that I have already spent the best parts of myself reaching it. The people I loved may have learned to live without me. The things that once brought me happiness may no longer feel familiar. I may finally hold victory in my hands and realise that I have no idea what to do with it.

I will continue to fight because I do not know any other way to live. I will lose on some days and still return on the next. But now, I carry a different fear with me—not merely the fear of failure, but the fear that I may eventually win and find nothing of myself left to save.

Perhaps that is the darkest cost of ambition: sometimes, by the time you conquer the life you wanted, it has already conquered you.

Written by Piyush Yadav
