My Fault, It Seems

Someone misplaced the moon last night,

And somehow, I was blamed for the tide.

The stars stood witness, dressed in white,

While truth found somewhere else to hide.

I was the shore that swallowed the sea,

The silent culprit of a storm unborn.

They carved their accusations into me,

Then wondered why my skin was torn.

I learned to speak in borrowed guilt,

To call each wound a debt unpaid.

Apologised for bridges others built,

And every ruin their departures made.

Perhaps I was the candle, not the flame,

Condemned for shadows on the wall.

Perhaps the world just needed someone's name

To give a reason to its fall.

And now I stand where verdicts bloom,

With neither evidence nor defence.

A garden growing from a tomb,

Where innocence itself's an offence.

How strange, to be both ash and spark,

The accused, the witness, and the plea.

They say I was the author of the dark—

My fault, it seems, for trying to see.

Written by Piyush Yadav
